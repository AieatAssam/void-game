## Assessment

The control changes follow the brief: fixed 60 ms steering, immediate neutral, captured touch pointers, preserved assist magnitude, constant planet travel in radii per second, and travel independent of audiovisual slow motion. Existing food ladders, recovery systems, edible weapons and world destruction already provide a strong game. The remaining release risks are actual rendered latency, balance after faster travel, and phone parity in the finale.

These are implemented working-tree changes, not completed acceptance evidence. The handoff records policy/build checks and earlier rendered failures; the latest warm-up and software-material revisions still miss the intended gates, and water needs motion review. Balance history changes intent: the later town replayability pass deliberately made play more forgiving and accepted more sloppy clears. Do not revive its earliest 2:45 floor or “sloppy mostly dies” target as a new requirement. Preserve fair challenge and flow: hunger, size eligibility, walls, route choice, damage, telegraphed dodge/dive decisions, rivals, recovery/seal and set-piece rewards should each remain meaningful without reducing steering responsiveness.

Large tear initialization is now bounded to 32,768 scan/merge operations per frame with texel-stage budgeting; very large waves can wait about a second for visible activation, which needs feel review on a constrained device. Region crumb scans are clamped to populated grid bounds; no frame-time gain has been measured for that edit yet.

Orbit, skip, results, restart and return-to-menu work with touch. Inspect near device safe areas, not just viewport edges.

Zero shader/runtime errors; no active-play compile/freeze above 100 ms. Loading warm frames may take longer while the loading UI remains responsive. Record renderer, pixel dimensions, CPU throttle and scene seed; powerful-Mac FPS is insufficient.
- **Fairness/pacing:** use existing seeds and at least three repeats per seed because director randomness produces large variation. The historical 19–26 min human / ≥20% naive gap was measured before controls removed movement slowdowns and is not a valid current target without recalibration: the latest one-run-per-seed human/naive sample was 353/371 s (+5%), while repeat samples varied widely. Repeat before tuning. Actual invariants: zero damaging attacks with <1.5 s committed warning, no single hit above 25%, mercy window still effective, recoverable damage and meaningful reasons to dodge/dive. Report each tier, losses, damage, travel share and recovery attempts. Compare T1 with its 4–5 min historical aim; investigate rather than automatically rebalance a changed completion time. Region: all eight documented human-suite seeds remain winnable; preserve observed 2:05–6:25 historical range as a comparison, not a rigid promise. Town: use the later forgiving replayability pass as the reference and confirm that careless play still incurs visible costs without requiring most runs to die.
- **Visuals:** paired before/after captures and 10 s motion clips at town/coast, regional capital, planet T1/T4/Moon, and finale peel/lens. On low/software settings, hole edge, edible targets, locked versus tracking threats and coastline remain distinguishable; water reads as a surface with depth and broad motion; no fine-detail shimmer or foam covering whole deep-water areas. Inspect grass-off composition and grade without bloom.

## Primary research basis

- [Chrome rendering guidance](https://web.dev/articles/rendering-performance) explains the 16.66 ms frame interval at 60 Hz and browser overhead, and separates continuous motion from discrete UI response. This supports measuring presented frames and input feedback separately. The numeric game gates above are proposed project targets.
- [MDN WebGL best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices) recommends smaller back buffers, batched draws and avoiding blocking calls. Apply these to pixel budgets and warm-up; quality flags alone are not performance evidence.
- [NVIDIA GPU Gems water chapter](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models) derives coherent wave height/orientation and separates geometric waves from texture detail. Retaining broad directional motion while filtering fine normals is the appropriate inexpensive water hierarchy here, an application of the source rather than a measured quality result.

Knowledge-vault check: Obsidian was unavailable. Direct searches found no hole-game project note; applicable fidelity, mobile-parity and terse-output preferences were read. No new vault note is proposed: findings above are repository facts or already recorded in the handoff.
